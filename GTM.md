# Go-To-Market Plan

## Exact Target User

Not "startups" — specifically: **engineering managers and CTOs at Series A or
pre-Series A startups with 5–30 engineers**, typically in India or Southeast Asia,
who approved AI tool subscriptions 6–12 months ago and have never audited them.

Secondary user: **freelance developers and final-year CS students** who pay
personally for Cursor or ChatGPT Plus and are not sure if they are on the
right plan.

Job titles: Engineering Manager, CTO, Tech Lead, Solo Founder with technical background.

## What They Google Right Before Wanting This

- "cursor vs github copilot which is better 2025"
- "chatgpt team plan worth it small team"
- "reduce AI tool costs startup"
- "github copilot alternatives cheaper"
- "how much does cursor cost per month team"

These are comparison and cost-evaluation searches — people already in the
buying/reviewing mindset. A blog post or tool that appears here catches them
at exactly the right moment.

## Where They Hang Out Online

- **r/ExperiencedDevs** and **r/cscareerquestions** — cost/tool discussions happen
  weekly
- **r/SideProject** and **r/entrepreneur** — founders sharing tool stacks
- **Hacker News** — "Ask HN: what's your AI tooling budget" threads get hundreds
  of comments
- **Indie Hackers community** — indiehackers.com, active Slack with #tools channel
- **Ship30for30 Discord** — writers and solopreneurs paying for AI writing tools
- **X (Twitter) lists** — "AI tools" lists followed by developers, easy to find via
  list search
- **Dev.to and Hashnode** — technical blog readers who share cost breakdowns

## How to Get the First 100 Users in 30 Days — Zero Paid Budget

### Week 1: Seeding (Days 1–7)

Post the tool on these specific places with a genuine "I built this" framing:

1. **Hacker News Show HN** — "Show HN: I built a free AI spend auditor for dev teams"
   Post between 9–11am EST on a Tuesday or Wednesday. Respond to every comment.
   Expected: 50–200 upvotes if the tool actually works, 20–40 direct signups.

2. **r/SideProject** — same post, different framing focused on the tool itself
   Expected: 10–20 signups

3. **Personal Twitter/X thread** — "I audited my AI tool spend and found I was
   wasting $X/month. So I built a tool that does this in 60 seconds. Free."
   Works best with a screenshot of a real audit result.

### Week 2: Earned Distribution (Days 8–14)

4. **DM 20 indie hackers** who have posted about their tool stacks.
   Message: "Hey, I saw your post about your AI stack — I built a free auditor
   that checks if you're on the right plans. Would you try it and tell me what
   you think?" Personalised, not spam.
   Expected: 5–8 responses, 3–5 genuine users who share if they find value.

5. **Post the shareable audit result** on Twitter — a real audit showing $X savings.
   The results page is designed to be screenshot-worthy. One good screenshot
   from a known developer can drive hundreds of visits.

6. **Dev.to post**: "We compared every AI coding tool plan so you don't have to"
   — a genuine comparison article that links to the tool. This gets indexed by Google
   and drives ongoing organic traffic.

### Week 3–4: Amplification (Days 15–30)

7. **Find one developer with 5k+ Twitter followers** who tweets about tooling costs.
   Offer to give them a personalised audit for free. If they share the result, that
   single tweet can drive 50–100 signups.

8. **Submit to ProductHunt** — schedule for a Tuesday. Have 10 friends ready to
   upvote and comment on launch day. ProductHunt front page = 200–500 signups
   in 24 hours.
